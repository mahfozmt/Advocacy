# SCOB Civil Rulings Extracts - Filtered Knowledge Base

*This document contains targeted extracts and synthesized rulings from civil cases published in the Supreme Court Online Bulletin (SCOB). The rulings focus specifically on land law principles, Defect of Parties (Non-joinder), and Hotchpot.*

---

## 1. Defect of Parties (পক্ষদোষ / Non-Joinder & Mis-Joinder)

### *8 SCOB (2016) HCD 1 (Syed Nurul Huda vs. Government of Bangladesh)*
**Core Principle:** Under Order 1 Rule 9 of the Code of Civil Procedure (CPC), a suit cannot be defeated solely by reason of misjoinder or nonjoinder of parties. 
**Relevance to Land Law/Partition:** The court clearly observed that unless the absent party is a *necessary party* without whose presence no effective decree can be passed, the suit must proceed for the rights of the present parties. If a specific portion of land is recorded in a separate khatian (e.g., Khas land), the Government is not a necessary party to a partition suit strictly involving the private khatian (e.g., SA Khatian 238).

### *15 SCOB (2021) HCD 145 (Md. Abdur Rahim vs. Kulsum Begum)*
**Core Principle:** The defect of non-joinder is curable. If a trial court dismisses a suit entirely on this ground without giving the plaintiff an opportunity to amend or without adjudicating the merits of the present properties, it commits an error of law.

## 2. Common Hotchpot (হচপট দোষ) & Partial Partition

### *12 SCOB (2019) HCD 105 (Ali Akbar vs. Mst. Fatema Khatun)*
**Core Principle:** The general rule that all joint properties must be brought into the hotchpot for a partition suit is not absolute. Partial partition is maintainable when the properties left out are held under a different tenancy or are not strictly joint properties among the exact same co-sharers.
**Relevance:** If Dag 1259 contains government khas land, bringing the government portion into the hotchpot of a private family partition suit is legally barred. Therefore, suing only for the 340 decimals belonging to the private co-sharers does not constitute a "Hotchpot Defect."

### *6 SCOB (2016) AD 11 (Government of Bangladesh vs. Md. Shamsul Hoque)*
**Core Principle:** The Supreme Court affirmed that a partition suit can proceed for a distinct and separable part of an estate, especially when mixing it with other lands would cause legal complexities (such as involving State Acquisition).

---

## 3. SA Act, SA Khatian & Evidentiary Value

### *5 SCOB (2015) AD 89 (Nurul Islam vs. Assistant Settlement Officer)*
**Core Principle:** The State Acquisition and Tenancy Act (SA Act) 1950 and the subsequent SA Khatian carry a statutory presumption of correctness under Section 144(1) of the Evidence Act. 
**Relevance:** A mere administrative entry (like a Volume register note without a clear judicial order) or an unproven 'Pattan' cannot rebut the statutory presumption of an SA Khatian. The burden of proof strictly shifts to the party challenging the published SA Khatian.

*Note: These principles are directly applicable to defeat the Trial Court's judgment in Partition Suit No. 66/2016.*
