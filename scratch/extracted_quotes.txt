Parties: Abdul Khaleque ------ Petitioner  Vs.  Shamsuddin and others ------ Respondents
Paragraph: 5. Mr. Muhammad Nazrul Islam, learned Counsel, appearing for the respondents submits that Mir Box the predecessor of the plaintiff, obtained pattan from Majumder Waqf Estate, the defendants also admitted in his chief that Mir Box obtained pattan from the Majumder Waqf Estate. He further submits that earlier the defendant filed Title Suit No.32 of 1984 and also filed Title Suit No.3 of 1991 that both the predecessor of the plaintiff and defendant obtained joint pattan from the Zaminder the defendant claimed that they were given pattan and the alleged pattan was obtained by the defendants predecessor and others but no documents were produced. He further submits that under Section 144A of the State Acquisition Tenancy Act an entry in the record of right has a presumptive value of its correctness. The defendant failed to produced any document of his kabuliyat as claimed. In support of his contention he cited the case of Modarres Ali and others vs. Bishambar Das being dead his heirs reported in 46 DLR 34, the case of Abdur Rahim vs. Tarequl Rahman reported in 50 DIAZ 66 and the case of Doyal Chandra Mondal and others-vs-Assistant Custodian vested and nonresident property reported in 50 DLR 186.

Ref: 1 ADC 387 | Case: Civil Appeal No. 168 of 2000
Parties: Shanti Ranjan Das ------- Appellant  Vs.  Khalilur Rahman Bhuiyan & others ------ Respondent
Paragraph: 10. The High Court Division held that on perusal of the bainapatra it appears that the Bengali Word 'Bhadra' and the English date 29.08.1980 have been inserted subsequently by different ink and by different hand in the place meant for writing the date of execution of the document and in the schedule R.S. Khatian No. 431 has been overwritten in place of C.S. No.41. The High Court Division further held that in suit for specific performance of a contract the plaintiff is required to come with clean hands to get the discretionary relief. The High Court Division further held that in the present case when insertion of both Bengali and English dates in relevant portion of the bainapatra by different ink and hand has been made and there are overwriting with regard to the schedule of the bainapatra, a great doubt is apparent as to the genuineness of the bainapatra and no discretionary   relief for specific performance of a contract can be granted on such bainapatra. The High Court Division further held that the trial Court considered this material evidence regarding the bainapatra and found that the bainapatra is not a genuine document. The appellate court below noticed that the trial Court found that there was overwriting in the bainapatra but did not mention in which page and in which line and which words have been overwritten without himself perusing the bainapatra and such sweeping remark of the final court of fact on a material question is not sustainable. The High Court Division further held that the court of appeal being the final court of fact was required to see for himself if there is any overwriting in the bainapatra which is the basic document of the plaintiff's claim. In the face of specific finding of the trial court in this respect the appellate court below totally ignored this material evident regarding the bainapatra Ext.1 and reversed the finding of the trial Court without himself perusing the evidence of the material document Ext. 1 and non consideration of this vital evidence by the final court of fact has resulted in a gross error of law which has occasioned a failure of justice.

File: case_12698_act__1950.json
Ref: 1 BLT (HD) 18 | Case: Appeal from Original Decree No. 27 of 1980
Parties: Fazlur Rahman ------ Appellant.  Versus  People's Republic of Bangladesh ------Respondent.
Paragraph: 11. Ext. 4(a) is certified copy of an order dated 8.9.77 passed by the Additional Deputy Commissioner (Rev), Dinajpur, in case No. 71/27 of 1976-77 under rule 23(4) of the Tenancy Rules made under section 152 of the State Acquisition and Tenancy Act. 1950. The present plaintiff was opposite party in this proceeding and he filed his pattani papers including dakhilas alleged to have been granted by the ex-landlords. In his order the A.D.C. (Rev) mentions that the first set of dakhilas showing settlement of 0.83 acres at a rental of Tk.1.06 by the ex-landlord appears to be genuine, but the second set of dakhilas showing settlement of 20.14 acres at a rental of Tk.14/- appears to be forged and produced recently. On examination the A.D.C. (Rev) found that S.A. Khatian No. 11 in respect of 0.83 acre of land was rightly recorded in favour of the opposite party but subsequently through manipulation 20.14 acres of land of different plots were brought into that khatian by increasing the rent to Tk. 15/-. On examination by the A.D.C (Rev). Registrar-II showed signs of manipulation by overwriting. The P.R.R. and Tahasil the copy of S.A. Khatian were examined and it was found that nine plots of khas land were included in Khatian No. 11 with different ink. It was further noted that though the opposite party (present plaintiff) paid rent for the khatian at the rate of Tk. 1.06 up to 1369 B.S. yet after this manipulation rent was again realised from him for the same holding from 1367 B.S. to 1370 B.S. On examination of the Office copy of the S.A. khatian of the C.O. (Rev) it was found that vanishing ink was used to erase those plots from the khas khatian and that handwriting of khatian No. 11 (disputed khatian) was entirely different from the handwriting of khatian Nos. 10 and 12. The A.D.C. (Rev) found Khatian No. 11 in the name of the opposite party (present plaintiff) which contains 0.83 acre at a rental of 11.1.06 but subsequently through fraud khas land measuring 20.14 acres of 9 plots were brought in Khatian No. 11 and accordingly plot Nos. 554, 694. 682. 500, 501, 474, 473, 472 and 554 fraudulently entered into khatian No. 11 were restored to Khatian No. 3 maintaining 0.83 acre of land in the name of the plaintiff in khatian No. 11 under rule 23 (4) the State Acquisition Rule. Under rule 23(4) the Revenue Officer is empowered to direct excision of fraudulent entry from the record of rights after final publication there of after consulting the previous settlement records, preliminary rent-rolls, draft record-of-rights. tahasil copies of records and Register-II.

This Court notes that this aspect of the Appellate Division's judgment in the Abdus Samad's Case has subsequently been relied upon in Zainal Abedin vs. Syed Hasmatullah and others, 4 BLD (1984), 272 to the effect that if a party seeking pre-emption fails to implead a party whose existence is disclosed upon a plea of non joinder having been taken, the right of pre-emption will be deemed to be lost. In Zainal Abedin's Case the matter was one of an initial objection as to a defect of parties and a petition filed thereafter specifically giving names and addresses of co-sharer tenants who were necessary parties but even then were not in fact impleaded. As a result, it was held that notwithstanding the submission of names and address of necessary co-sharers long before the hearing of the case, and there being no evidence of any waiver of the objection as to omission of necessary parties, failure to take steps to implead such co-sharers as parties proved fatal to the pre-emption case.
