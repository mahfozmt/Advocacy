Mirza Hussain Haider J. This Civil Appeal, by leave, is directed against the judgment and order dated 07.01.2010, passed by a Single Bench of the High Court Division in Civil Revision No. 1034 of 1998, discharging the Rule and thereby affirming the judgment and decree dated 1.7.1997 passed by the learned District Judge, Gopalgonj, in Title Appeal No. 92 of 1996 reversing those dated 24.8.1996 passed by the learned Subordinate Judge(now Joint District Judge), 2nd Court, Gopalganj in Title Suit No. 67 of 1995 dismissing the suit.

2. Facts necessary for disposal of this Civil Appeal, in brief, are:

It is stated in the plaint, inter alia, that originally the land of schedule No.1 property belonged to Abhoy Charan Haider and Debendra Nath Haider with 16 gondas each and Pulin Behari Haider, Shaibalini Haider and Sarju Bala Haider having 10 gandas, 2 Kara and 2 Kranti each. Similarly land of schedule Nos. 2-5 properties belonged to Abhoy Charan Haider and Debendra Nath Haider in 4 (four) annas share each and Pulin Behari, Shaibalini and Sarojini @ Sarju Bala Haider had 2 annas, 13 gands, 1 kara and 1 kranti share each. Surja Bala died leaving behind only daughter Kanak Prova who, entered into a contract to transfer 3.56 acres of land to Samiruddin, the predecessor of the plaintiffs, upon receipt of TK. 1,000/= out of total consideration money of TK.1500/- on 21.10.1952 and subsequently on receipt of the balance consideration filed by defendant Nos. 1 and 2 the Revenue Officer of Muksudpur submitted a report to the Additional Deputy Commissioner (Revenue), Faridpur, on 30.03.83 and on the basis of the said report the names of the defendant Nos. 1 and 2 were mutated vide order dated 19.4.1983 in respect of the suit land. 9. The trial Court, on examination of the materials on record and on hearing all the parties dismissed the suit on contest by judgment and decree dated 24.08.1996 whereupon the plaintiffs preferred Title Appeal No. 92 of 1996 before the learned District Judge, Gopalganj, who allowed the appeal in part and thereby decreed the suit declaring that the plaintiffs have got title and possession in respect of 3.29 acres of land on the basis of their purchase vide kabala dated 04.02.1954(Exhibit-2) and accordingly, allotted saham to the plaintiffs for the said quantum of land (3.29 acres of land) by his judgment and decree dated 1.7.1997. Against the said judgment and decree of reversal, the defendant’s No. 1 and 2 moved the High Court Division in Civil Revision No. 1034 of 1998 and obtained Rule. The High Court Division, on hearing the Rule, dis-charged the same by the impugned judgment and order dated 07.01.2010, observing that in Title Suit No. 4 of 1955 filed by the present defendants for permanent injunction against Samiruddin, (the predecessor of the plaintiffs), Court found Samiruddin's purchase of 3.29 acres of land from Konok Prova by kabala dated 4.2.1954 was valid and the said finding having been affirmed by the appellate Court as well as by the High Court Division the same is still subsisting. Moreover the SA record also stands in the name of Samiruddin and after his death in the names of the plaintiffs'.

10. Being aggrieved by the aforesaid judgment and order of the High Court Division the defendants preferred Civil Petition for Leave to Appeal No. 174 of 2011 before this Division and obtained leave which gave rise to this appeal.

11. From the leave granting order it appears that leave has been granted to determine the point as follows: "Because out of 3.29 acres of land covered by the disputed kabala deed No. 1436 dated 04.02.1954 only 1.99 acres of land of RS Khatian Nos. 33, 44 and 30 are included in the schedule of the plaint of Title Suit No. 67 of 1995, the plaintiff-respondents were/are not entitled to get preliminary decree for more than 1.99 acres of land but without examining the contents of the kabala an area of 3.29 acres has been given in the preliminary decree."

12. Mr. Quamrul Haque Siddique, the learned Advocate appearing on behalf of the defendant-appellants submits that though the sale deed dated 04.02.1954 contained 3.29 acres of land on six different khatians but all those six khatians have not been brought in the hotch-pot of the schedule of the plaint as a result the plain-tiffs are not entitled to get saham for the entire 3.29 acres of land sold through the said Kabala. He submits that out of the six khatians incorporated in the said deed, Khatian Nos. 19, 42 and 428 containing 1.30 acres of land have not been included in the hotch-pot of the suit, rather only Khatian Nos. 33, 44 and 30 containing total 1.99 acres of land have been incorporated in the schedule of the plaint and saham has been sought for only to that extent. Since all the khatians described in the Kabala, which covers 3.29 acres of land have not been brought within the botch-pot of the plaint and as such the plaintiffs are not entitled to get saham to the extent of entire 3.29 acres of land described in the said kabala. Thus the reasons given by the High Court Division as well as by the Court of appeal below giving saham to the extent of 3.29 acres of land, which is the entire subject matter of the kabala, in favour of the plaintiff-respondents is erroneous. Hence this appeal should be allowed.

13. It appears that the plaintiff respondent No.1 filed caveat but did not file any concise statement in this appeal. Thus under the Appellate Division Rules the arguments advanced by the learned Advocate-On-Record on behalf of the plaintiff respondent No.1 cannot be taken into consideration.

<u>14. From the record, it appears that other than defendants No. 1 and 2 none challenged the saham given in favour of the plaintiff-respondents to the extent of 3.29 acres of land. It also appears that the ownership of Konok Prova is admitted by both the parties and the present defendants namely, Abhoy Charan, Debendra Nath and Pulin Behari, earlier instituted Title Suit No. 4 of 1955 for permanent injunction against Samiruddin which was dismissed with cost by the Court below holding that Samiruddin's, (predecessor of the plaintiffs), purchase of 3.29 acres of land from Konok Prova by Kabala dated 4.2.1954 was valid and genuine. The said finding having not been challenged has not been set aside by any of the higher Courts. When a competent Court found the kabala of the predecessor of the plain-tiffs is valid and the said finding is still in force now it is difficult to hold that the predecessor of the plaintiffs is not entitled to get saham to the extent of 3.29 acres of land which he purchased from Konok Prova by registered deed dated 4.2.1954. This status of the plaintiffs has been con-currently found by the court of appeal below as well as by the High Court Division.</u>

15. Even then the trial Court dismissed the suit on the ground that the deed of the predecessor of the plaintiffs was registered subsequent to deed of the predecessor of the defendants although the plaintiffs produced the baina patra dated 21.10.1952 pursuant to which the deed was registered on 4.2.1954 was an earlier document. The whole case of the plaintiffs is that Konok Prova never executed any deed in favour of the predecessor of the defendants. The Court of appeal below, as the last Court of fact, on consideration of the entire facts and circumstances found the deed of the plaintiffs dated 4.2.1954 to be valid and held that the plaintiffs proved their title in respect of 3.29 acres of land transferred by Konok Prova. This finding of the court of appeal below has been affirmed by the High Court Division. While considering this aspect the Court of appeal below also considered the judgment and order passed in Title Suit No. 4 of 1955 filed by the defendants, wherein the present defendants failed to prove their claim of prima facie title and possession over 3.29 acres of land of the suit land. For that reason, the Court of appeal below decreed the present suit giving saham to the extent of 3.29 acres of land, (which is the subject matter of the deed of the plain-tiffs dated 4.2.1954) to the plaintiffs. The High Court Division also affirmed the said finding of the Court of appeal below. The defendants, now, before this Division, has come up with a new plea that all the six khatians contained in the deed of the plaintiffs dated 4.2.1954 has not been incorporated in the hotch-pot of the suit. Thus they are 'entitled to get saham to the extent of land which they have incorporated in the schedule of the plaint only i.e. only to the extent of 1.99 acres and the land remaining in three other khatians (khatian Nos. 19, 42 and 42 of the deed) covering 1.30 acres of land having not been incorporated in the hotch-pot of the suit they are not entitled to get saham to that extent. Considering the facts and circumstances of the case it is prayed that if the plaintiffs at all get any saham they will at best be entitled to 1.99 acres of land not the entire property purchased by their predecessor vide the said deed. To determine this question we need to examine the plaint, as well as the deed.

16. The trial Court framed a particular issue being issue No. 4 which reads as follows :

“অত্র মোকদ্দমা হচপট দোষে অচল কি?”

<u>17. While discussing the said issue the trial Court specifically found that though the defendants claim that the suit should be dismissed because of the fact that the entire property has not been incorporated within the hotch-pot of the case but on perusal of the five schedules described in the plaint it found that all the plots have not been brought within the hotch pot of the suit and thus held that the suit and thus held that “বাদীগণ খরিদ সমস্ত জমি বাবদ বন্টনের প্রতিকার প্রার্থনা না করিয়া আংশিক ভুমি দাবী হইতে বাদ দিলে উহার দ্বারা সমস্ত হচপট ভূক্ত করা হয়নাই বুঝায় না। প্রশ্ন হইতেছে, যে খতিয়ানের জমি বাদীগণ দাবী করিতেছেন ঐ খতিয়ানের সাকুল্য ভূমি হচপট ভূক্ত করা হইয়াছে কিনা? এই প্রসঙ্গে বিবাদী পরে বিজ্ঞ কৌশুলীগণ কিছুই বলেন নাই। তাহারা শুধুমাত্র বাদীগণ সমস্ত দাবী করেন নাই এই বক্তব্যের উপরই তাহাদের যুক্তি সীমাবদ্ধ রাখিয়াছেন। ১/২ নং বিবাদীগণ নালিশী খতিয়ান সমূহের আর, এস পর্চা দাখিল করিয়াছেন যাহা প্রদর্শনী ঙ১-ঙ১(৪) হিসাবে চিহ্নিত হইয়াছে। উক্ত খতিয়ান সমূহ পর্যালোচনায় খতিয়ানের সাকুল্য জমি হচপট ভুক্ত করা হইয়াছে মর্মে সিদ্ধান্তে আসা যায়।</u>

<u>���তএব, মোকদ্দমাটি হচপট দোষে অচল নহে।”</u>

18. This being the specific finding of the trial Court and affirmed by the appellate Court and the defendants without challenging this finding before any Court of law, now has come forward with the plea that though the Court of appeal below as well as the High Court Division gave 3.29 acres of land as saham to the plaintiffs which was purchased by the predecessor of the plaintiffs vide deed dated 4.2.1954 but the entire property described in the schedule of the said deed has not been brought within the hotch-pot of the suit.

19. On examination of the schedule annexed to the plaint as well as those incorporated in the deed of sale executed by Konok Prova on 4.2.1954 it appears that schedule Nos.1 to 5 to the plaint relates to Khatian Nos. 30, 33, 41, 44 and 461 respectively. On comparing the same with the khatians described in the schedule of the deed by which the plaintiffs claim their title, it appears that schedule No.2 of the said deed covers .98 acre of land in khatian No. 33, schedule No.3 of the deed covers .23 acre of land of Khatian No. 44, schedule No.4 of the deed covers .78 acre of land within Khatian No. 30, schedule-5 of the deed covers .39 acre of land of Khatian No. 42 and schedule-6 covers .03 acre of land of Khatian No. 428 which appears to have been transferred to the predecessor of the plaintiffs.

20. So it appears that by schedule-1 to the plaint (which-attracts Khatian No. 30), the plaintiffs sought saham in respect of 1.58;1 acre of land whereas they got only .78 acre of land by the said deed in question. By Khatian No. 33 described in schedule 2 to the plaint, the plaintiffs claim for 5.59 acres of land whereas the deed shows in respect of Khatian No. 33 the predecessor of the plaintiffs purchased from Konok Prova only .98 acres of land. In schedule-4 to the plaint, the plaintiff claims 1.28 acres of land within khatian No. 44 but the kabala shows that the predecessor of the plaintiffs purchased only .23 acre of land in the said khatian by the said deed.

21. So it is clear that although the plain-tiffs prayed for saham to the extent of 9.241-t acres of land described in five schedules of the plaint but actually the plaintiffs appear to have purchased 3.29 acres of land from the aforesaid schedules of the deed in six khatians. Interestingly the plaintiffs did not bring three out of six khatians of the deed in the hotch-pot of the suit and as such the plaintiffs did not pray for saham to the extent of 1.30 acres of land as described in Khatians No. 19, 42 and 428. Under such circumstances Mr. Siddique submits that the plaintiffs can at best get saham to the extent of 1.99 acres of land only from Khatian Nos. 33, 44 and 30 as brought in the hotch pot of the suit which their predecessor purchased from Konok Prova not beyond that. If this contention of Mr. Siddique is accepted then conclusion may be drawn to the effect that the deed dated 4.2.1954 though has been proved to be true and valid but the plain-tiffs are not entitled to get saham to the extent of the whole of the property transferred by the said deed i.e. 3.29 acres of land as they have not incorporated 1.99 acres of land from three khatians, as mentioned above, of the said deed in the hotch pot of the suit.

<u>22. It is required to be mentioned here that the plaintiffs claimed 9.24 acres of land in five schedules of the plaint out of which, 3.29 acres of land has been purchased by their predecessor from Konok Prova by deed dated 4.2.1954 and the rest of the 5.95 acres of land is the land which their predecessor purchased through auction in Execution Case No. 12 of 1956 for realization of the cost awarded in Title Suit No. 4 of 1953. Though the plaintiffs also claimed saham for the said auction purchased land but the Court of appeal below as well as the High Court Division did not grant any saham in respect of that auction purchased land and the plaintiffs did not take any step against such finding. As such we shall have to confine ourselves within the subject matter of kabala dated 4.2.1954 which the Court of appeal below found to be genuine and valid and affirmed by the High Court Division.</u>

<u>23. From the discussions made herein-above it appears that the plaintiffs' title to the extent of 3.29 acres of land has been proved before the court of appeal below who granted saham to the said extent and allowed the appeal in part which has been affirmed by the High Court Division. But admittedly in the schedule of the plaint the plaintiffs did not incorporate all the khatians described in the schedule of the said deed. Unless the saham is sought for in respect of any particular property, even if the Court holds that the plaintiffs have title, it cannot grant saham to the extent which the plaintiffs did not bring within the hotch pot. Therefore, we are of the view that the plaintiffs' saham to the extent of 1.99 acres of land in Khatian Nos. 33, 44 and 30 has been properly given in their favour. But since 1.30 acres of land from Khatian Nos. 19, 42 and 428, though has been transferred in favour of the predecessor of the plaintiffs by deed dated 4.2.1954, and they have title therein but they having not incorporated the same in the hotch pot of the suit and thereby ask for saham for the same the same cannot be granted in this particular partition suit. As the cause of action of a suit for partition is admittedly a recurring cause of action whenever a party or a co sharer of a case jote feels that he requires partition and the defendant refuses to give partition he can file a suit for partition claiming saham to that extent. Thus the plaintiffs may take appropriate steps as and when requires in respect of the rest of their saham which were not incorporated in the schedule of the plaint.</u>

24. With these observations this civil appeal is disposed of granting saham of 1.99 acres of land in Khatian Nos. 33, 44 and 30 in favour of the plaintiffs.

25. There will be no order as to cost.
